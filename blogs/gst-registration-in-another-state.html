How to Get GST Registration in Another State Without an Office

GST
2026-10-28 · 8 min

Step-by-step: getting a separate GSTIN in a new state using a virtual place of business.

If your business already has a GSTIN in one state and now needs to operate in another, you cannot simply add the new state to your existing number. GST registration in other state is a fresh application for a separate GSTIN, linked to the same PAN. The good news is that you do not need to rent a full office to do it. A virtual place of business (VPOB) with proper address documents is a common, practical way to register, as long as the premises are genuine and you are ready for verification.

Below is a step-by-step walkthrough of how to apply for a GST number in another state, the documents you will need, and how a VPOB fits in.

When you need a GSTIN in another state

GST is registered state-wise. Once you have a place of business in a state, meaning a location from which you supply, store goods, maintain records or carry on business, you generally need a registration in that state. Typical triggers include:

  • Storing stock in a marketplace fulfilment centre located in another state.
  • Opening a branch, sales office or service centre in a new state.
  • Wanting to invoice local customers or bid for tenders from within that state.
  • Supplying from that state rather than shipping everything from your home state.

Businesses that only make occasional supplies in a state for a short period may have the option of registering as a casual taxable person instead, which has its own conditions, including paying an advance deposit of estimated tax. For ongoing operations, a regular registration is usually the right route. Thresholds and special rules for certain sellers can change, so confirm your position with a chartered accountant.

What a VPOB is and how it helps

VPOB stands for virtual place of business. In practice it means using a virtual office address, in a real commercial premises, as your principal place of business in the new state. The provider gives you a rent agreement, a No Objection Certificate from the owner and a recent utility bill, which together form the address proof the GST portal asks for. They also receive your mail and can cooperate if a GST officer visits.

Ecommerce sellers often hear VPOB together with APOB, the additional place of business. A common pattern is to use a VPOB as the principal place of business in a state and add the marketplace warehouse there as an APOB, because the stock is physically held at the warehouse. Documents required for GST registration in other state

The exact list depends on your business type, but for a company, LLP, partnership or proprietorship registering in a new state you should usually keep these ready:

  1. PAN of the business, which must be the same PAN used for your existing GST registrations.
  2. Proof of constitution, such as the certificate of incorporation for a company or LLP, or the partnership deed for a firm.
  3. PAN, Aadhaar, photograph, mobile number and email of the proprietor, partners or directors.
  4. Details and photograph of the authorised signatory, with a board resolution or authorisation letter appointing them.
  5. Address proof of the principal place of business in the new state: a rent or lease agreement, or a NOC from the owner, plus a recent utility bill such as an electricity bill.
  6. Address proof for any additional place of business in that state, such as a warehouse.
  7. Bank account details, which may be required at application or soon after registration depending on current portal rules.

Make sure the address on the rent agreement, NOC and utility bill matches exactly, and that the agreement and NOC are in the legal name of the business. Mismatched spellings and documents in a director's personal name are among the most common reasons for queries.

How to apply for GST number in another state: step by step

  1. Secure your place of business in the new state, either leased premises or a VPOB, and collect the address documents.
  2. On the GST portal, start a new registration application, choose the new state and enter your business PAN, mobile number and email. Verify with the OTPs to receive a temporary reference number.
  3. Log in with the temporary reference number and complete the application: business details, promoters, authorised signatory, principal place of business, any additional places of business, and the goods or services you deal in.
  4. Upload the documents in the formats and sizes the portal accepts.
  5. Opt for Aadhaar authentication for the promoters and authorised signatory, and complete it through the link sent to their registered mobile numbers or email.
  6. Sign and submit the application using a digital signature certificate (required for companies and LLPs) or EVC where allowed. You will receive an application reference number to track the status.
  7. Respond to any clarification notice from the officer within the time given, and be prepared for physical verification of the premises.
  8. Once approved, download your registration certificate. The new GSTIN starts with the code of the new state but carries the same PAN.

The government does not charge a fee for GST registration on the portal itself; costs come from the address, professional help and digital signatures. Processing times vary by state and by whether verification is ordered, so build in a buffer rather than counting on a fixed date.

After registration: staying compliant

  • File returns for the new GSTIN separately and on time, including nil returns when there is no activity.
  • Raise proper tax invoices or delivery challans for stock moved between your own registrations in different states, as these transfers are generally treated as supplies.
  • Display your GSTIN and business name as required at the place of business.
  • Keep the address agreement active for as long as the registration uses that address, and file an amendment on the portal if the address changes.
  • Update marketplace seller accounts, bank records and invoices with the new GSTIN.

GST rules and portal steps are updated from time to time, so check the latest guidance on the GST portal or with your CA before applying.
