The 2026 Guide to Multi-State GST Registration

GST
2026-04-28 · 8 min

Everything you need to register for GST in every state you sell in.

Selling across India is easier than ever, but GST still follows state lines. If your business operates from more than one state, you will usually need GST registration in multiple states, with a separate GSTIN for each. This guide walks through when multiple GST registration in different states is required, the documents required for GST registration, the application process on the GST portal and the mistakes that most often lead to queries or rejections.

Why GST registration is state-wise

GST in India is a destination-based tax shared between the Centre and the states. To keep track of which state is owed what, registration is tied to the state where you have a place of business. A single PAN can hold several GSTINs, one per state or union territory, and each GSTIN is treated as a distinct person for GST purposes. That means separate returns, separate invoice series and separate input tax credit ledgers for every registration.

Within one state, you do not need a new GSTIN for every office or warehouse. Instead, one location is declared as the principal place of business and the others are added as additional places of business on the same registration. A new GSTIN is only needed when you set up a place of business in a different state.

When you need GST registration in multiple states

Whether you need a second or third GSTIN depends on where your business actually operates, not just where your customers are. Common triggers include:

  • Opening a branch, sales office or service centre in another state.
  • Storing inventory in a warehouse in another state, including marketplace fulfilment centres such as Amazon FBA or Flipkart warehouses.
  • Supplying services from a location in another state where your team is based.
  • Bidding for contracts or vendor empanelment where the client prefers or requires a local GSTIN.
  • Wanting to make intra-state supplies to customers in a state so that CGST and SGST apply instead of IGST.

Simply shipping goods from your home state to a buyer in another state generally does not require registration in the buyer's state; that is an inter-state supply billed with IGST from your existing GSTIN. Separately, inter-state suppliers of goods have generally been required to register regardless of turnover, but thresholds and exemptions change from time to time, so confirm your position with a CA or on the GST portal.

Documents required for GST registration

The exact list depends on your business type, but for each state where you apply, you should be ready with the following:

  • PAN of the business (the same PAN is used for every state).
  • Proof of constitution, such as the certificate of incorporation for a company, the LLP agreement or partnership deed.
  • PAN, Aadhaar, photograph and contact details of the proprietor, partners or directors, and of the authorised signatory.
  • Authorisation letter or board resolution appointing the authorised signatory.
  • Proof of the principal place of business in that state, typically a rent or lease agreement with an NOC from the owner, plus a recent utility bill such as an electricity bill.
  • Details and proof for any additional places of business in that state.
  • Bank account details; depending on current portal rules this may be added at application or shortly after approval.

Address proof causes more problems than anything else. The name and address on the rent agreement, NOC and utility bill must match each other and the address entered on the portal, down to the floor and unit number. Documents should be legible, signed and recent.

Step-by-step: applying for an additional state GSTIN

  1. Secure a place of business in the new state, whether a branch office, warehouse or a properly documented virtual office.
  2. Collect address proof in the name of your business for that location.
  3. Start a new registration application (Form REG-01) on the GST portal using the same PAN, selecting the new state.
  4. Complete OTP verification for the email and mobile number of the authorised signatory.
  5. Fill in business details, promoters, authorised signatory, principal place of business, goods and services, and upload documents.
  6. Complete Aadhaar or biometric authentication as the portal requires; in some cases this may involve a visit to a GST facilitation centre, and applications can also be flagged for physical verification.
  7. Respond promptly to any clarification notice raised by the officer through the portal.
  8. Once approved, download the registration certificate and display it, along with the business name board, at the place of business.

Processing times vary by state and by whether physical verification is ordered. Plan for some buffer before you need to start invoicing from the new state.

Using a virtual office for a new state

Many businesses do not need a full office in every state they register in. A service company may only need a local presence for clients, and an ecommerce seller may only need a principal place of business in a state where their stock sits in a marketplace warehouse. In these cases, a virtual office can act as the principal place of business in that state, provided the paperwork is genuine and the premises can be verified.

Bear in mind that GST officers can physically visit the declared address. The premises should be a real commercial space, your business name should be displayed, and someone at the location should be able to confirm your arrangement. A provider that cannot support verification is a risk to your registration.

Compliance after you register in more than one state

Each GSTIN carries its own compliance calendar. Before you expand, make sure you can handle:

  • Separate periodic returns for each GSTIN, even in months with no sales from that state.
  • Stock transfers between your own registrations, which are generally treated as supplies and need proper invoices or delivery challans.
  • Cross-charge or input service distributor arrangements for common expenses, where applicable.
  • Reconciling marketplace reports with returns for each state.
  • Keeping the address, signatory and bank details on each registration up to date through amendments.

Missed returns can lead to late fees, blocked e-way bills and eventually cancellation, so only add states you genuinely need and can maintain.
